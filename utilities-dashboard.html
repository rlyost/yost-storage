What drives the electric cost

  • Evenings are the biggest hours. Use climbs from 4 PM and tops out at 9 PM (2.5 kWh/hr in winter, 3.3 in summer). The 9 PM hour falls outside every plan's peak window, and E-TOU-D's peak is only 3 weekday hours (vs. 5 hours every day on E-TOU-C), which is why E-TOU-D comes out cheapest.
  • High base load. Overnight (1–4 AM) use averages 1.0–1.7 kWh an hour. That always-on load alone adds up to about 25–40 kWh a day, most of the 39 kWh daily average. Finding always-on loads (well/pool pump, heaters, old fridge/freezer, servers) is the biggest savings lever.
  • August 2026 was the highest month (1,612 kWh), with the top day on 2026-08-09 (99.9 kWh). Worth asking what ran that week.
  • Moving use out of weekdays 5–8 PM (dishwasher, laundry, dryer) saves the most per kWh on E-TOU-D.
